# Limitations the FOOTAGE made unavoidable (no engine change recovers these)

These cap the achievable score regardless of how good the editor becomes. A
skilled human editor with these same 10 clips hits the same walls.

1. **No close-up shots anywhere.** Every clip is a distant wide from roughly one
   standing position. The subject is a small figure in a large frame
   (frame-verified across hook/build/peak). "Cinematic workout" requires
   close-ups of strain, grip, feet, face — none were filmed. Hard ceiling on
   emotional intensity and action visibility.

2. **Single camera position / no angle variety.** No reverse angles, no low
   angles, no overhead, no tracking. Shot-variety is structurally impossible;
   the critic's `enoughShotVariety=false` cannot be won from this footage.

3. **No camera movement.** All static. No push-ins, no follows, no handheld
   energy. Movement/energy must come entirely from subject motion in-frame,
   which is small because the framing is wide.

4. **Repetitive activity.** ~80% tire flips; the remainder is tire drags,
   walking, and brief stretching. A genuine intensity ARC (warmup → build →
   peak → cooldown) was not performed/filmed, so it cannot be assembled.

5. **No dedicated establishing or reflection footage.** No intentional
   scene-setting shot, no payoff/exhaustion moment shot for that purpose. The
   template's location/reflection beats had nothing purpose-built to draw from.

6. **Audio is conversation, not effort.** Two people talking over the session;
   no isolated grunts, tire impacts, or footfalls. Natural-sound value is
   inherently low; the pipeline correctly does not fabricate SFX.

7. **All one lighting/location/time.** Good for continuity (a strength — the
   edit's one high score, 7/10), but offers zero visual variety to cut between.

8. **Long, unbroken takes.** Continuous phone recording with no in-camera cuts,
   so all segmentation is synthetic (~8s windows). Fine for analysis, but means
   there are no naturally-composed "shots," only slices of one long wide take.

Consequence: even a perfect editor is bounded to roughly a competent
*session-recap* from this material — not a cinematic piece. Estimated human
ceiling on THIS footage: ~5–6/10 (see doc 04). Breaking past that is a **capture
problem**, addressed by the Project Two plan (doc 07).
